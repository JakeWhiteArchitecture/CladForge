1 · Model

No model loaded. Nothing is uploaded: the file is parsed in your browser.

A state file holds every chain, the wall surfaces it is attached to, and every setting. It loads without the IFC; open the IFC first to see the host model, since opening a model starts afresh. Load state replaces the job; Add states adds the chains of one or more saved states to the job that is open, each chain keeping its own settings. A name already in use gets the next free one, and a wall face the job already has is left out.

2 · Elevations

None hidden

Click a wall face to add its coplanar patch to the active elevation; click again to remove it. Click a face round a corner and it becomes the next elevation in the chain, with the coursing carried round. Faces that don't meet the chain start a new one. Openings punched by the importer become holes.

Stuck or misbehaving? Run cladforge() in the browser console for the engine state and a log of every extraction.

Picking generates nothing on its own. When the chain is complete, press Enter or use Make chain at the top right of the view to choose the cladding and build it. After that the whole panel edits live.

3 · Buildup

A client's model can carry its own battens, as columns set out to suit the frame behind. Where a face has them, the vertical layer goes on their centrelines (the battens, or under vertical planks the counter-battens) and the offset slider has nothing to move. Panel joints land on them, and each window's reveal line is a joint carried straight up and down past it, on a batten above and below the opening.

The default for chain corners; each corner, jamb and head can be set on its own (the corner rows above, or click its edge in 2D). Mitred cuts the whole buildup on the corner's bisector. Master is a panel detail: one board runs out flush to the other's face and the other stops a joint gap clear of it. A standing seam chain reads Seam (a seam stood on the corner, on one face: Swap on the corner row moves it to the other) and Flashing strip (the trays meet as if mitred under a strip) in place of Master and Profile. Profile is for right-angled external corners and window and door reveals: an aluminium outer corner profile with panels, and with planks a Marley-type external corner profile (a 31 mm nose, its 34 mm flanges flush with the planks' backs), the planks butting it. Square is a placeholder.

Openings

Panel edges land on the window jambs and each span between them is split into equal bays no wider than the maximum panel, so a window that does not suit the panel centres still sets the joints. A solid timber cavity closer goes to both vertical sides of every opening. Jambs and heads are lined; sills are not.

Edges for the active chain

Offsets pull the battens, counter-battens and boards back from the top, bottom and free-end edges. Chain corners and window or door jambs take no offset; where boards are mitred the mitre gap opens between them. In the 2D view the edges are drawn in these colours: click one to type its value. Every value applies to the whole chain.

4 · Panels

The cut sizes are the panels as set out; the board is the stock they are cut from, so changing one keeps the other. Edge trim (below) squares up factory edges before any piece is cut. The waste readout, top right of the view, packs every panel of every built chain onto boards, each elevation on its own (kerf between pieces, offcuts reused); Optimise boards there runs the full search, and Download cutting plan always uses it.

Panel width drives batten centres: joints land on a batten, intermediate battens keep spans under 600 mm. Horizontal joints are open joints at the gap. Noggins behind them are only generated where counter-battens are on: between vertical battens a noggin sits on the drainage plane and dams it.

4 · Planks

A fixed plank with its real section. Draw your own as a DXF: the section as a closed polyline, two or more copies at the spacing they are laid at (that spacing is the cover; a DWG needs saving as DXF first). The face is the side with more of the outline on it. Planks are set out as lapped boards, the section's full width at its cover; while a slider is dragged they are drawn as plain boards.

Opaque metal trays a seam-centre wide on a continuous deck, on vertical battens so the cavity drains whichever way the trays run. Each seam is a closed double lock (25 or 38 high), both trays rolled together with 1.8 of air in every fold: head about 11 across, 17 deep; clips are not modelled. A seam runs only where two trays meet: where it runs out it is folded down to the tray at 45°, or stops square, flush with the tray's end, set per chain under Edges, top and bottom ends apart. With Set out from the structural openings on, vertical trays put a seam on each reveal corner, running the full height up past the window, the reveal lining locked into it as the second tray. The metal stands 2 off the deck and off the reveal. Cross joints follow the plank rules below, anywhere on the deck. The stock length is the longest tray. Full-length trays runs every tray its whole run in one piece, cut from the coil, so no tray is laid above another and there are no cross joints; the length fields below then do not apply.

Fewer More3600 mm

Lap > 0 courses at face width minus lap (lapped profiles); lap = 0 courses at face width plus gap (open-jointed). Courses start at the splash zone and work up. End joints land on battens and stagger course to course. A run is set out in pieces of the length shown beside the slider, never longer than the stock, and no splice leaves a piece shorter than the shortest piece: only a run shorter than that is one short piece. The waste readout and cutting plan pack the pieces onto stock lengths, each elevation on its own.

Cutting

Off, every board is cut for one elevation. On, each elevation cuts what it can from the offcuts the elevations before it leave (in chain order), wherever that buys fewer boards, and keeps those pieces together on the wall. The cutting run shows each elevation's offcuts gathered at its end.
